Featured · COMPENG 3DY4 · Winter 2026 · Team of 4

Real-Time FM Software-Defined Radio

An FM receiver built in software. It takes raw I/Q samples from an RTL-SDR dongle and produces mono audio, stereo audio and Radio Data System (RDS) data in real time on a Raspberry Pi 4. It supports four sample-rate modes, and the filter length scales from 75 to 101 taps as the CPU clock is lowered from 1.5 GHz to 600 MHz.

What I built

  • The C++ stereo path: mixer, sample-rate-conversion filtering, stereo combiner, and the PLL and delay-block functions that recover the 38 kHz carrier and keep the mono and stereo channels aligned.
  • The C++ mono path for modes 0 and 1.
  • Multithreading: split the RF front end and audio processing into producer and consumer threads, joined by a thread-safe queue (mutex and condition variable).
  • Loop unrolling of the FIR convolution, the main hot spot in the signal chain.
  • The RDS Python model from Manchester decoding through the application layer, which decodes the station's PI code, program type and program-service name.

Hardest bug: the left and right channels kept bleeding into each other. The cause was the mono delay block, which was being delayed by taps × U instead of taps / 2. That put mono about 30 ms out of phase with stereo and wiped out the channel separation.

Featured · COMPENG 3DQ5 · Fall 2024 · Team of 2

FPGA Image Decompressor

Hardware, written in SystemVerilog with my partner Lisa Chtcherbakova, that turns a compressed 320×240 image back into RGB pixels on an FPGA. The compressed image arrives over UART into external SRAM, and the decoded pixels are written back to SRAM for the VGA controller to display. We completed and verified Milestone 1 (upsampling and colour-space conversion) and partially completed Milestone 2 (the inverse DCT).

  1. UART in
  2. SRAM
  3. Lossless decode M3 (not implemented)
  4. Inverse DCT M2 (partially complete)
  5. Upsample + YUV→RGB M1 (complete)
  6. VGA out

Complete Partial Not implemented

What I built

  • Milestone 1, co-designed and co-written with my partner. A six-clock-cycle "common case" turns out one even/odd RGB pixel pair using three shared multipliers. It repeats 160 times per row for all 240 rows, overlapping Y, U and V SRAM reads with packed RGB writes.
  • Interpolation that exploits symmetry. Odd U′/V′ samples come from a 6-tap filter (coefficients 21, −52, 159). Mirrored samples (j±1, j±3, j±5) are summed before multiplying, which halves the multiplies, and they come from a sliding window of six U and six V registers.
  • Row-edge handling. Six special-case codes clamp the filter window at the start and end of every row, so edge pixels reuse the nearest valid sample.
  • Colour conversion with clipping. Fixed-point YUV→RGB accumulates over several cycles, then each channel is saturated to 0–255 before two channels are packed into each 16-bit SRAM write.
  • Milestone 2, my part. I worked on the logic that fetches 8×8 blocks of S′ from SRAM into dual-port embedded RAM, and on the first matrix multiplication (T = S′·C) using three multipliers and a cosine-coefficient lookup table. My partner took the second stage.

How we worked: we planned every clock cycle in a spreadsheet state table before writing any SystemVerilog: which multiplier computes what, when each SRAM read lands, and when each write goes out. In the final schedule, all three multipliers are busy in five of the six cycles. We then derived the Milestone 1 latency by hand and confirmed it in simulation (4,608.28 µs, or 20 ns per cycle at 50 MHz). We traced the critical path from a multiplier input to the result_Go accumulator through 17 interconnect hops.

Capstone · Sep 2025 – Apr 2026

Early-Stage Wildfire Detection Network

A solar-powered wireless sensor network that catches wildfires at the smouldering stage. Nodes with a ~100 m range detect smouldering temperatures (~300 °C) and relay heat level and location toward a lookout tower, while drones forward packets and imagery to a ground station.

  • nRF52 firmware for sensor sampling, local processing, buffering and packet transmission over the integrated BLE radio.
  • BLE 5.0 Mesh messaging over connectionless advertising packets, which avoids power-hungry persistent connections under dense canopy.
  • Solar energy-harvesting stage: a boost charge controller stores low photodiode-array output in a supercapacitor, enough for several transmissions per charge.
  • Sensor fusion: IR thermopile, electrochemical CO sensor and a pulsed-LED optical smoke sensor.

Pacemaker Firmware & Device Controller-Monitor

Pacemaker firmware for an NXP FRDM-K64F board, modelled in Simulink and Stateflow, plus a Python desktop app (the Device Controller-Monitor, or DCM) that programs and monitors it over serial. It supports eight pacing modes: AOO, VOO, AAI, VVI and their rate-adaptive versions.

  • Built the DCM's GUI on my own in Tkinter: patient login and registration (up to 10 local users), a screen for each of the eight pacing modes with range-checked parameter entry and saved settings, and a live electrogram (egram) plot of atrial and ventricular signals.
  • Worked on the Simulink pacing functions: Stateflow pacing logic generated into firmware for the K64F, with rate-adaptive modes driven by the board's FXOS8700 accelerometer.
  • The DCM talks to the board over a packed binary serial protocol at 115200 baud. It writes parameters, then reads them back to confirm what the pacemaker stored.

COMPENG 2DX4 · Microprocessor Systems Project

3D Spatial Mapping Device

An embedded device that maps a space in 3D. A time-of-flight distance sensor on a stepper motor sweeps a full circle to measure one cross-section. Moving the device forward between sweeps builds up slices, which software on a PC stitches into a 3D point cloud.

  • Firmware for a Texas Instruments MSP432E401Y microcontroller (MSP-EXP432E401Y LaunchPad) that steps the motor, reads the time-of-flight sensor over I2C, and streams the distance readings to a PC over UART.
  • Python on the PC that turns each distance and motor angle into x, y, z coordinates and renders the scan in 3D.
  • The scan shown here is real device output from a hallway in McMaster's Engineering Technology Building (ETB): 4 sweeps of 32 readings each (one every 11.25°), taken 300 mm apart.
